# Intentional Design Principles

Use these principles as lenses for decisions, not as a formula or a visual theme. A strong review asks whether the product's choices are purposeful, coherent, humane, and carefully executed.

## The doctrine

### Purpose before pixels

Start with the human outcome. State the primary job of each journey and the reason each visible element exists. Features cost attention, time, and trust; elements that do not support the purpose must be clarified, subordinated, consolidated, or removed. A visually perfect screen that solves the wrong problem is not well designed.

### Agency and trust

People should understand what is happening, what will happen next, and how to recover. Inspect status, feedback, permission rationale, reversibility, destructive consequences, escape paths, and error recovery. Do not trap people in modes or make them gamble with their work.

### Responsibility and harm prevention

Consider everyone affected, including non-users. Minimize data collection, expose uncertainty, protect sensitive contexts, and anticipate abuse, exclusion, manipulation, unsafe defaults, and downstream harm. AI-mediated experiences must communicate limits and support human review or recovery where consequences matter. Constrain or remove a feature when its foreseeable risks outweigh its value; visual polish cannot redeem irresponsible behavior.

### Familiarity and coherence

Build on concepts and platform conventions people already understand. Elements that look alike should behave alike. The same action should keep a predictable label, placement, style, and result unless context justifies a difference. Coherence is end-to-end: hardware, software, content, motion, sound, and service behavior should feel considered as one experience.

### Flexibility and inclusion

Design for varied abilities, inputs, devices, languages, environments, and preferences from the beginning. Preserve context as the interface adapts. Accessibility is part of quality, not an optional compliance pass.

### Simplicity means exactly enough

Simplicity is not visual emptiness. Reduce friction, unnecessary choices, steps, jargon, redundancy, and hidden essential actions. Add context when it helps people make an informed decision. Every element earns its place, but necessary information must not be buried to make a frame look minimal.

### Craft earns confidence

Care is visible in shared edges, consistent gaps, optical balance, precise type, clear wording, crisp assets, responsive feedback, fluid motion, stable layout, and reliable behavior. Pixel precision matters when it expresses a system or matches a reference; arbitrary one-pixel opinions do not. Inspect the parts people rarely notice as carefully as the obvious hero moments.

### Delight is a consequence

Choose the emotion appropriate to the product and reinforce it through useful motion, content, sound, and character. Delight should emerge from clarity, agency, reliability, and care. Decoration that delays a task or harms accessibility is not delight.

### Quality requires iteration

Treat shipping as a checkpoint. Test in realistic contexts, invite fresh eyes, discard weak ideas, repair regressions, and maintain the system as platforms and needs evolve. A review is complete only when its recommendations have verifiable acceptance criteria.

## Applying the philosophy without imitating Apple

- Judge the product against its purpose, intended audience, native platform, brand, design system, and supplied references.
- Never prescribe Apple's palette, San Francisco type, symbols, glass treatments, corner language, spacing, or component styling solely because Apple uses them.
- Preserve distinctive brand expression when it remains legible, coherent, accessible, and purposeful.
- Prefer platform conventions for learned behavior and accessibility, not for cosmetic mimicry.
- Label objective evidence, system inference, and expert judgment separately.
- Do not reduce the doctrine to a single score. Design principles can conflict; explain the tradeoff and the reason for the recommendation.

## Historical continuity and source notes

Source set last reviewed on 2026-08-30. Paraphrase these sources; quote only when the exact wording and provenance matter. Recheck current standards or product guidance when a later audit depends on their exact requirements.

- [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles) is the current canonical statement of purpose, agency, responsibility, familiarity, flexibility, simplicity, craft, and delight.
- [Principles of great design, WWDC26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/250/) defines design as intentional, distinguishes simplicity from minimalism, and connects craft with detail, performance, and continued care.
- [Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines) and its guidance on [layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), [typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography), [color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), [motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion), and [accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) provide concrete platform examples. Treat those examples as Apple-platform baselines, not universal styling rules.
- [Essential Design Principles, WWDC17](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2017/802/) emphasizes human-centered wayfinding, feedback, visibility, consistency, grouping, and mental models.
- [Intentional Design, WWDC18](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/802/) connects authentic empathy, direct labels, predictable information architecture, and deliberate choices.
- [The Qualities of Great Design, WWDC18](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/801/) describes quality as considered rather than random and uses inconsistent physical gaps as an example of visibly weak craft.
- [Design foundations from idea to interface, WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/359/) connects product purpose, information architecture, navigation, hierarchy, components, and progressive disclosure.
- The [Steve Jobs Archive, Make Something Wonderful](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/) preserves Jobs's emphasis on bridging sophisticated technology and ordinary human use. A [1997 WWDC closing chat](https://allaboutstevejobs.com/videos/misc/wwdc_1997_closing_chat) is available as a non-Apple secondary archival copy and supports the themes of starting with customer experience and focus through saying no; corroborate exact wording before quoting it.
- Apple's [2012 organization announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2012/10/29Apple-Announces-Changes-to-Increase-Collaboration-Across-Hardware-Software-Services/) and Apple-published [How Apple Is Organized for Innovation](https://www.apple.com/jobs/pdf/HBR_How_Apple_Is_Organized_For_Innovation-4.pdf) support integration across disciplines, expertise, collaborative debate, and immersion in detail.
- [Designed by Apple in California chronicles 20 years of Apple design](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2016/11/designed-by-apple-in-california-chronicles-20-years-of-apple-design/) documents Apple's stated continuity of care and human motivation after the Jobs era.
- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) is the standards baseline for web accessibility; apply relevant native-platform guidance for non-web products.

## Attribution cautions

- The complete BusinessWeek 1998 statement commonly shortened to “design is how it works” includes appearance, feel, and operation. If used, attribute it to the dated interview rather than treating the shortened slogan as the whole philosophy.
- Do not attribute “simple can be harder than complex” to the 1998 BusinessWeek interview without a primary scan.
- Do not present “innovation is saying no to 1,000 things” as the verified wording of the 1997 WWDC session. The reliably supported point is that focus requires saying no.
- Avoid mythology that credits one individual for the work of Apple's multidisciplinary teams. Use the historical material to illuminate principles, not to imitate a personality.
